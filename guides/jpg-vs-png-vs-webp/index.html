JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Image Format Should You Use?

Updated October 24, 2026 · ImageTools4Free

Almost every image you see online is a JPG, a PNG or a WebP file. They can look the same on screen, but they work in different ways, and choosing the wrong one can make a file several times bigger than it needs to be or make a logo look fuzzy. Here is what each format is good at.

Lossy and lossless: the key difference

Lossless compression, used by PNG, makes a file smaller without changing a single pixel. When you open it again, it is exactly the same as before.

Lossy compression, used by JPG and usually by WebP, makes files much smaller by simplifying details your eyes are unlikely to notice, such as tiny changes of colour in a blue sky. You choose how much to simplify with a quality setting. The catch is that the removed detail is gone for good, and saving a JPG over and over slowly lowers its quality.

JPG (JPEG)

JPG has been the standard format for photos since the early 1990s. It handles the smooth colour changes in photos very well and produces small files. Every phone, camera, computer, browser and website supports it, which makes it the safest choice when you are sending a picture to someone else or uploading it to a form.

JPG is weak with sharp edges and flat colours. Text, line drawings and logos can get blurry "halos" around the edges. It also cannot store transparency, so any see-through area becomes a solid colour.

PNG

PNG keeps every pixel exactly, supports transparent backgrounds, and keeps text and edges perfectly crisp. That makes it the right choice for logos, icons, screenshots, diagrams and any graphic you will edit again.

The downside is size. Because nothing is thrown away, a photo saved as PNG is often three to five times larger than the same photo as a good-quality JPG.

WebP

WebP was created by Google for the web. It can work in a lossy mode like JPG or a lossless mode like PNG, and it supports transparency in both. For photos, WebP files are usually about 25–35% smaller than JPG at a similar quality, which makes web pages load faster.

All current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari display WebP. Some older programs, photo printers and upload forms still do not accept it, so if a site rejects your WebP picture, convert it to JPG with our Format Converter.

Which one should you choose?

  • Sharing a photo, emailing it or uploading it to a form: JPG.
  • Photos on your own website or blog: WebP, or JPG if you need the widest support.
  • A logo or icon with a transparent background: PNG, or WebP for websites.
  • A screenshot with text: PNG.
  • An image you will keep editing: PNG, so it does not lose quality each time you save.

Converting between formats

You can switch any picture between JPG, PNG and WebP with the Format Converter. Keep in mind that converting a JPG to PNG does not bring back detail that JPG compression already removed; it only stops further loss. And converting a transparent PNG to JPG fills the see-through parts with white.

How to compress an image

  1. Click the upload box, or drag a picture onto it. JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP files all work.
  2. Move the Quality slider. 70–80% is a good starting point for photos.
  3. Pick the output format: JPEG for the widest support, or WebP for smaller files at the same quality.
  4. Press Compress image. You will see the original size, the new size and how much you saved.
  5. If you are happy with the result, press Download image. If not, change the quality and try again.

Choosing the right quality

Quality controls how much detail the compressor is allowed to throw away. At 90% and above, the file is usually close to the original and the saving is small. Between 60% and 80%, most photos look the same to the eye but the file is often half the size or less. Below 50%, you may start to see blocky patches in smooth areas like skies and skin.

A simple method is to start at 75%, compress, and look at the preview closely. Lower the quality step by step until you notice a change, then go back up one step.

JPEG or WebP?

JPEG works everywhere: every browser, phone, email program and photo app can open it. WebP is a newer format made by Google that usually gives a file 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality. All modern browsers support WebP, so it is a great choice for images on your own website. If you are sending a picture to someone or uploading it to a form, JPEG is the safer choice.

Why did my file get bigger?

This can happen when the original is already well compressed, or when it is a PNG with large flat areas of colour such as a screenshot or logo. Re-saving such a file as JPEG can add size instead of removing it. Lower the quality, or try WebP. For screenshots with text, the Format Converter to PNG sometimes gives better results.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Image Compressor really free?

Yes. There is no limit on how many images you can compress, no signup and no watermark added to your pictures.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Compression happens inside your browser on your own device. Your images are never sent to our servers.

Will compressing remove transparency from my PNG?

If you choose JPEG, transparent areas become white, because JPEG does not support transparency. Choose WebP if you need to keep transparent areas.

What is the best size for website images?

For most websites, a photo under 200 KB loads quickly. Resize the image to the width it will be shown at first, then compress it.
